The one-field defect

The Contrarian and the Researcher were given different mandates, no shared context, and no ability to see each other's work. Both independently found the same error in the same field, and both proposed the same fix. That is the highest-confidence finding this exercise has produced in either round.

Contrarian · Researcher · independent agreement

"Month/year of service" breaks the entire zero-PHI thesis

HIPAA Safe Harbor at 45 CFR 164.514(b)(2) requires stripping all elements of dates except the year for dates directly related to an individual — and date of service is explicitly named. §5.1 of the PRD collects month and year. That single field means the data is not Safe Harbor de-identified, before any argument about re-identification.

The Researcher pushes it further: payer + code + CPT + amount + service date is the textbook definition of a Limited Data Set, which is a defined category that remains PHI, requires a Data Use Agreement, and — when the recipient uses it for the covered entity's health care operations, which is what triage is — folds those terms into a BAA. So "no PHI, therefore no BAA" is not a gray area. It is a citable miss on a named field.

Contrarian

The scrubber upgrades a disclaimer into a warranty

V1's waiver was a disclaimer — it shifted risk. V2's client-side scrub is an affirmative technical representation, and a regex will not catch "services provided to J. Smith" or a non-standard member ID. The first paste that gets through means you have both received PHI without a BAA and told customers in writing that this could not happen. That is deceptive-practices territory, which unlike HIPAA has no covered-entity gatekeeping.

The fix, per both reviewers

Collect year only. Compute the filing deadline client-side from a denial date that never leaves the browser. Delete the free-text paste box rather than scrubbing it — the Buyer independently called it "risk theater that reintroduces the exact doubt you engineered away."

Two of the three changes are one-line. With them the zero-PHI position becomes defensible; without them the product is a HIPAA violation sold to therapists as a compliance feature.

What v2 introduced or left standing

Researcher

The free tier is already free, at least six times over

Free CARC/RARC lookups with plain-English fixes exist at CureAdvantage, ClarixHealth, Lerasign, PUREDI, a free Chrome extension, and — most damagingly — PayerReady, which gives away 308 CARC plus 1,198 RARC codes with appeal templates, root causes and prevention tips. Nobody charges because the underlying X12 code list is a public standard that every frontier model has already memorized. X12 also updates it quarterly, so the "deterministic table" is a permanent treadmill, not a one-time build.

Researcher

The $39 packet is a competitor's content-marketing loss leader

Muni Health already publishes per-payer appeal deadlines, submission routes, portal-vs-form guidance and letter templates as free indexed pages. EZAppeal does the AI letter at $3, first one free, and is already running the payer × CPT play into mental health with psych-testing appeal guides. Two of the biggest payers also have no form to sell: Cigna has no standard provider appeal form, and UnitedHealthcare's mechanism is the portal, not a downloadable PDF.

Contrarian · Researcher · independent agreement

The distribution channel is the worst query class in 2026 search

"What does CO-197 mean" is a definitional informational query — near-universally answered inline by AI Overviews, with the large majority of those searches ending in no click at all. And payer × code × CPT is template-with-variable-substitution by definition, the exact pattern named in the March 2026 scaled-content-abuse enforcement, where affected page sets lost the majority of their traffic with no manual action and no notification. The plan's one viable acquisition channel is aimed into both problems at once.

Logician · Contrarian, with arithmetic

The outcome ledger is arithmetically unreachable

A win rate needs roughly 30 observations for a ±18-point confidence interval and ~96 for ±10. The cell space is payers × codes × arguments — several hundred to several thousand cells — traffic is Zipfian, and day-35 response on a one-off purchase with no account runs maybe 20%. The Logician's arithmetic: ~5,000 paid appeals, or roughly $195k of revenue, before a single cell says anything true. Worse, responders self-select toward wins, so the first numbers published would be biased upward — on indexed pages, to sell packets.

Contrarian

BILL THE PATIENT is a liability grenade on the free, unauthenticated path

A deterministic table cannot know network status, contract terms, or whether the payer miscoded a group code — CO claims arriving as PR is routine. Telling an in-network therapist to bill a patient for what was actually a contractual adjustment induces a payer-contract violation and possible balance-billing exposure. Same for a wrong deadline: contractual timely-filing windows are invisible to you, public policy says 180 days, their contract may say 90, and one missed window posted in a 40,000-member therapist group ends distribution permanently.

That last line is the whole problem, and it is not a pricing problem. As the Logician put it: the plan's economics and the founder's timeline are describing two different companies. The one channel that pays inside the runway is the one all three bears pointed at.

The cheapest 48-hour test

The round-one gate — 20 therapists, 3 pay $40 for a hand-written appeal — should still run, but three reviewers independently flagged that it validates v1's hypothesis, not v2's. It is warm, hand-sold and human-delivered; self-serve converts far worse. A pass proves less than it appears to, and the Contrarian's warning is the one to hold onto: if it passes, the temptation will be to read it as permission to build the software.

Run this alongside it. It costs phone calls and answers the question that actually gates the next twelve months.

  1. Call thirty mental-health billing services

    Not therapists. The layer above them — the companies charging 4–10% of collections who eat denials for a living, already hold BAAs, and have the claim volume a solo practice never will. Ten relationships instead of a thousand customers, and they can be reached by phone this week.

  2. Ask two questions and nothing else

    What would you pay for win-rate data on your own denials, broken out by payer and code? And would you pay me to work the appeals you currently write off? The first tests whether the ledger has a buyer before you can build it. The second tests whether there is a service business here right now.

  3. Ask the therapists you hand-write for one extra thing

    Email them at day 45 — not 35, per the Buyer, because a reconsideration is often still pending — with paid / denied / not yet. The response rate to that one email is the single variable the entire ledger thesis rests on, and you can measure it before writing any code.

What the answers mean

If billers will pay for the data or the service, that is the business — build there, where the volume, the BAAs and the money already are, and let the therapist-facing tool be the sensor that feeds it. If the day-45 response rate comes back in the single digits, delete the ledger from the pitch and the practice tier from the roadmap, and ship only the free deadline-and-worth-chasing tool with a thin $39 branch. That is a small, honest, real product. It is not a company, and you should know which one you are building before you spend the runway.

If you reshape again

  • Month → year

    One-line change. Compute deadlines client-side from a date that never leaves the browser, and delete the paste box instead of scrubbing it. Do this first — everything else is downstream of the legal claim being true.

  • Answering → arrival

    The Buyer's whole remaining objection: "the denial has to find me, I will not go find it." Without ERA detection, a five-minute EHR integration, or a forward-the-denial-email address, a twice-a-month tool gets forgotten. Solve arrival, not answering.

  • Solos → billers

    Named independently by all three bear-side reviewers as the first move. They have the volume, the BAAs, and a budget. The therapist tool becomes the funnel, not the business.

  • Four verdicts → real instructions

    RESUBMIT CORRECTED and CALL PAYER are the most frequent outputs and the least actionable — one names a class of fix, the other admits ignorance. Ship the 3–5 most common causes of this code on this CPT, and for CALL PAYER the provider line, the exact question, and what to have ready.

  • Ledger → out of the pitch

    Build the write path — three tables, one day — because the data is worth having. Take the win rates out of the pitch, the roadmap and the indexed pages until a cell is actually significant, which is thousands of appeals away.

  • $39 → $25–29

    The Buyer named $25–35 in round one and got $39: "at $25 I click without thinking; at $39 I do arithmetic, and arithmetic is where purchases die." A competitor is at $3. Drop the three-pack — asking someone with 24 denials a year to pre-commit is the subscription problem in a smaller box.
